HEIMDALL Mission Concept

A Sun-Earth L4 heliophysics observatory and Earth-Mars communications relay designed for the 2026 AIAA Undergraduate Space RFP.

  • AIAA 2026 Undergraduate Space RFP Response
  • Illinois Institute of Technology

HEIMDALL is a complete mission proposal developed for the 2026 AIAA Undergraduate Space RFP. The concept places a spacecraft at the Sun-Earth L4 point to provide heliophysics observations and an Earth-Mars communications link, helping characterize solar events and reduce communications gaps for future Mars missions.

The project carried the design from requirements definition and mission operations through payload, communications, propulsion, power, structural, thermal, and command-and-data-handling trade studies. The final architecture combines a science instrument suite with heritage X-band and Deep Space Optical Communications, a Falcon 9-enabled heliocentric transfer, and a 15-year operating concept while meeting the competition’s $400 million cost constraint.

2026 AIAA Competition Brief

Undergraduate Team Space Design Competition: Heliophysics Science Observer & Communication Platform.

  • Mission goalObserve heliophysics and space-weather events while supporting dependable communications for future Earth-Mars missions.
  • Required proposalDefine a complete mission architecture with operations, subsystem trades, technical analyses, budgets, schedule, risk, and requirement compliance.
  • Program constraintsKeep the mission within a $400M budget, launch before 2036, and support operations into the late 2040s.
  • EvaluationDemonstrate technically sound analysis, clear communication, originality, and a feasible, practical design.

What I Did / Learned

  • My work focused on sizing HEIMDALL’s station-keeping propulsion system for its 15-year Sun-Earth L4 mission. I established the station-keeping delta-v budget by considering L4’s gravitational stability, the maneuver cadence needed for laser communications and coronagraph observations, and solar-radiation-pressure disturbances. I also incorporated the Falcon 9 second stage into the mission architecture for the high-delta-v phasing transfer, reserving the spacecraft propulsion system for on-station operations.
  • I evaluated Hall-effect, monopropellant, and bipropellant options against propellant mass, power demand, impulse time, system complexity, heritage, and integration risk. The analysis selected the Northrop Grumman MRE-5.0 monopropellant thruster: it provided the best balance of 19.01 kg of propellant, 232 s specific impulse, responsive maneuver capability, and straightforward integration. I documented the trade study and linked the final recommendation to the mission’s mass, power, cost, and operational requirements.
